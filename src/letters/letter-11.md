title: "The Witness You Carry"
---

I met your friend recently, and I want to tell you what I saw: someone real. Not polished, not performing — real. He was caught off guard and couldn't collect himself fast enough to hide what was actually happening, which meant I got to see it. The blush that spread across his face was the best thing about the meeting. It came from somewhere deep, and it told me that whatever is good in him hasn't been managed into smoothness yet.

Hold onto that about him, and hold onto this: the blush won't disappear when he becomes wiser. Some things are bodily and stay bodily, no matter how thoroughly the mind is trained. The finest speaker sweats before a crowd. The steadiest man trembles at the knees. Pompey, who commanded armies, reddened whenever he faced a public gathering. Wisdom tones these things down; it cannot remove them. They are nature's signature on us, and nature doesn't negotiate.

This is actually good news. It means the goal isn't to become someone who feels nothing — someone smooth and unmoved and perfectly controlled. That person doesn't exist, and the attempt to become them is its own kind of damage. What we're after is someone who feels what they feel and acts rightly anyway, whose blush is still there at eighty because it was always part of them.

Here is the practice I want to leave you with, because I've found nothing more useful: choose someone to carry in your mind. Not an ideal, not an abstraction — a person. Someone whose life, conversation, and face have satisfied you. Someone in whose presence you would want to be your best self.

Then live as if they're watching.

This sounds like performance, but it isn't. What it actually does is give the conscience a shape to work with. The soul needs someone to respect — someone whose imagined presence makes even the private moments more hallowed. The question *what would they think of this?* is more effective than almost any resolution you could make alone, because it's immediate and specific where resolutions are general and fade.

> The soul should have someone whom it can respect, one by whose authority it may make even its inner shrine more hallowed.

Choose carefully. The model should be someone you could actually become, not someone so remote they only inspire despair. If the most severe choice seems too far, choose a gentler spirit. What matters is that they're real to you — that you can picture the face, hear the voice, feel the standard they set by simply being who they are.

You'll know you've found the right person when you notice that thinking of them makes you want to straighten up.

Yours, still watching and still learning from the watching,
