title: "Nobody Owes You Tomorrow"
--- 

I have to tell you about someone I knew — not intimately, but enough to feel it. He'd built himself up from nothing, and he was careful, the way people are when they remember what it cost to have nothing. He knew how to make money and how to keep it, which are two different skills and most people only ever learn one. He spent an entire day at a dying friend's bedside, came home, ate a good dinner, and by morning his own throat had closed on him and he was gone. Hours. That's the whole gap between a full life and no life at all.

I keep thinking about the version of him that existed the day before — mid-deal, mid-plan, mid-everything, certain there'd be a later to cash it in. There wasn't. There rarely is a guarantee of later, and we build our whole lives as if there is.

You do this too. I've watched you talk about the life you'll have once the current mess clears — once the job settles, once the money's steadier, once you've earned the rest you're promising yourself. I do it as well, so I'm not lecturing from outside it. But I don't think rest is something you earn on a delay. I think it's something you take now or you don't take at all, because the delay isn't guaranteed to end in your favor.

> There's a limit fixed somewhere ahead of both of us. Neither of us knows how close it is.

Here's what I actually believe, though it took me a long time to be able to say it without flinching: the only life that isn't wasted is the one that doesn't need tomorrow to be complete. Not because tomorrow won't come — it probably will — but because a life built entirely on drawing against the future is a life that's never actually lived, just perpetually financed. Pay it off daily. Let each day close clean, like it could stand on its own if nothing followed it.

I think about the people who beg to keep living no matter the cost — who'd take any deformity, any pain, any diminishment, just to keep breathing one more stretch of time. I understand the fear underneath it. I don't understand the trade. Because if you're asking to suffer longer just to avoid ending, you've already answered the only question that matters, and answered it wrong. The point was never how long. It was whether the thing you lived was worth calling a life.

I'm not writing this because I think either of us is close to the edge. I'm writing it because I don't want to wait until one of us is to say the true things. So: I love you, plainly and now, not as a plan for later.

Today, whichever one this is —
