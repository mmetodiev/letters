title: "Your words are the only portrait I have of you."
---


Every time you write to me I feel it — the particular quality of your presence arriving through the words. Not a substitute for being together, but something real in its own right. The way a person writes, the rhythm of it, the things they choose to say and not say — it's a kind of fingerprint. I'd know yours anywhere.

Which is why I want to say something about how you speak, and how you write. Not to correct you — you don't need that. But because I think you already sense this and haven't quite let yourself trust it yet.

Speed is a form of hiding. I've watched people talk — brilliant people, people with genuinely important things to say — and sometimes the torrent of words is really just a way of not being examined. If you move fast enough, no one can pin anything down. Nothing can be questioned because nothing fully lands. The words impress, but they don't enter.

> Speech that is meant to heal has to stay in the system. A remedy that passes through too quickly does nothing.

The same is true in the other direction — stammering slowness loses people just as surely. But between those two failures there is a pace that is neither performing nor hiding. Deliberate. Measured. Words chosen because they're the right ones, not because they're available.

There's something else underneath this. When you speak carefully — when you slow down enough to mean what you say — you can't easily say things you don't believe. Speed lets you outrun your own conscience. Slowness holds you accountable to yourself in real time. That's uncomfortable. It's also the point.

> How you speak is how you think. And how you think is who you are becoming.

So my simple ask: be slow. In writing, in conversation, in the moments when you feel the pull to fill silence with noise. The words that have been waited for sink deeper than the ones that fly past. You have things worth saying. Let them arrive at the pace they deserve.
