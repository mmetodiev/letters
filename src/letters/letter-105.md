title: "What Actually Keeps You Safe"
--- 

I want to give you something practical this time, not a meditation — think of it as advice for keeping the house standing, not a philosophy of houses. Here's what drives people to hurt each other: hope for gain, envy, hatred, fear, and contempt. Once you can name them, you can start defending against each one specifically, instead of just bracing generally against the world.

Contempt is the mildest of the five, oddly enough. Someone who looks down on you will wound you once and move on — nobody hunts what they've already dismissed. It's the people who see you as a rival or a threat who circle back. So envy is next, and the defense there is simple, if unglamorous: don't make yourself a target by broadcasting what you have. Keep your good fortune quiet. People covet what catches their eye, even small things, if you insist on holding them up to the light.

Hatred usually comes from one of two places — either you provoked it, which you can just... not do, or it arrived unprompted, which no amount of good behavior fully prevents. Some people get hated without ever having made an enemy. That one isn't fully in your control, and I want you to know that now so it doesn't blindside you later.

> Being feared is its own trap: the man who inspires fear lives afraid himself, and no one has managed both at once.

As for fear — don't be the kind of person others feel they need to handle carefully. Stay easy to offend without being dangerous to offend, and quick to forgive without looking weak for it. Being feared costs you as much peace as it seems to buy you in leverage.

But here's the piece I actually want you to hold onto, because I think it matters more than the rest combined: talk less. Not out of secrecy for its own sake, but because there is something almost drugging about conversation — it loosens things in us we didn't mean to loosen, the way a second drink does. Nobody keeps a secret at the size they received it. Everyone has one person they trust completely, and that person has one person too, and by the third telling your private thing has a name attached and a life of its own. Say less. Sit with yourself more. You are better company than you give yourself credit for.

And underneath all of it — the real security, the kind no strategy replaces — is simply not doing the things that would give you something to hide. People who live carelessly are never at rest, because guilt keeps interrogating them long after the act is done. You don't need to get away with something. You need to not need to get away with anything. That's the whole difference between someone who sleeps well and someone who merely hasn't been caught yet.

Watching out for you, as always —
