chapterNum: 81
title: "Give Anyway"
--- 

I want to talk about generosity — not the easy kind, where the other person is grateful and you feel warm about it afterward, but the kind where you give and nothing comes back, and you have to decide what that means about you.

You've been burned. I know. Someone you helped didn't return it, or returned it badly, or worse — turned the help into a grievance, as though receiving from you had become a debt they resented. It happens more than it should. And the reasonable response, the self-protective response, is to close down a little, to give less freely, to audit your generosity before you extend it.

I want to argue against that response. Not because it isn't logical, but because of what it costs you.

> Even after a poor crop one should sow again. Refusing to plant because last season failed doesn't protect you — it just guarantees the famine.

Here is what I've come to believe: gratitude is not primarily something the other person owes you. It's something you owe yourself. The person who gives and then lets go — who doesn't keep a ledger, who doesn't brood over what wasn't returned — that person is free in a way the careful, guarded giver never is. The ungrateful person torments themselves, Seneca says. They shrink the gift in memory and magnify the injury. They poison themselves with their own resentment. You don't have to do that.

The reward for giving well is having given well. That's not a consolation prize. That's the whole thing.

> No one who has benefited another has failed to benefit themselves — not because the favour will be returned, but because of what generosity does to the person who practices it.

I'm not saying absorb unlimited harm. There's a difference between generosity and submission. But the basic orientation — toward giving, toward trusting, toward interpreting ambiguous acts kindly rather than sourly — that orientation is worth protecting. It's part of who you are. Don't let a few ungrateful people talk you out of it.

Give anyway. Not for them. For you.

With love, and meaning every word —
