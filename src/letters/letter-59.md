title: "The Difference Between Pleasure and Joy"
---


You said something in your last message that made me genuinely happy — not the polite kind, not the kind that evaporates on the drive home. I've been sitting with that distinction ever since, trying to get it right.

Pleasure is what you feel when something good lands. Joy is what you live in when you've built something true. They can look identical from the outside, but one of them disappears when the circumstances change, and one of them doesn't.

> Pleasure needs a cause. Joy doesn't — or rather, it is its own cause. That's how you tell them apart.

Here's what I've noticed about myself, and I suspect it's true for you too: I've spent a lot of time pursuing the feeling without doing the work that produces the durable version of it. The applause, the good night, the hit of being understood — I wanted those, and I got them sometimes, and they were real, and then they were gone. The problem wasn't wanting them. The problem was mistaking them for the destination.

The version worth building is quieter. It doesn't spike. It doesn't require the right circumstances or the right person in the room. Seneca calls it living on a plane with the gods, which sounds grandiose, but I think he just means: not at the mercy of the next thing.

I also want to say this, gently: we are not good at knowing when we're being flattered into complacency. Someone tells us we're doing well, and we relax in a way we haven't earned. Alexander was certain he was Jupiter's son right up until the arrow. The wound is the honest voice.

> Don't let anyone — including me, including yourself — talk you out of the work you still have to do.

I'm telling you because I love you, and because I need to hear it too.

Yours, in something closer to joy than I used to manage —
