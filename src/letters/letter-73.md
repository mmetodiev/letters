title: "You Already Have What God Has"
--- 

I want to tell you something that might sound strange at first, so stay with me.

There's a kind of person who withdraws from the noise — from the meetings and the jostling and the endless performance of ambition — not out of bitterness, but in order to do something more important with the time. And that person, Seneca says, is the one who owes the deepest thanks for peace. Not the ones grinding away inside it, barely noticing it. The one who actually *uses* the quiet knows what the quiet is worth.

That's you, in the best version of yourself. And I want you to own that.

> The truly ambitious man never looks behind him at how far he's come — only ahead at whoever is still in front. That is not a life. That is a treadmill with a view.

What strikes me about the way you move through the world is that you do notice — the conditions that make thought possible, the people who hold things steady so others can go deep. That noticing is rarer than it sounds. Most people treat peace the way they treat oxygen: invisibly, until it's gone.

Here is the thing Sextius said that I keep returning to: Jupiter has no advantage over a good person except time. The god lasts longer. But the quality of the goodness — the actual texture of living with integrity and clear eyes — that's the same. What god *has*, the wise person simply doesn't *want*. Which means, in the only sense that matters, they're even.

> Divine seeds are scattered in us. What grows depends entirely on what kind of ground we are.

I believe you're good ground. I've watched you receive things — hard things, surprising things — and I've seen what you do with them. You don't bolt them down like a dog catching scraps. You hold them, turn them over, let them mean something. That's not nothing. That's almost everything.

The path to whatever we want to call the highest life isn't long or complicated. Thrift, self-restraint, courage. That's the whole list. You're already on the road.

With admiration, honestly,
