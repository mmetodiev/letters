title: "While the House Is on Fire"
---

I want to tell you something I've noticed about myself, and I suspect it's true of you too: I am capable of spending enormous amounts of time on questions that are genuinely interesting and genuinely useless, while the actual work of my life waits.

I've been doing it this week. I've been turning over a distinction — whether wisdom is a Good and being wise is also a Good, or whether only one of them qualifies — with the kind of focus I should be giving to my fear, my grief, my habits that haven't changed despite years of knowing they should. The distinction is subtle. I cannot tell you that it matters.

No one running to fetch help for a daughter in labor stops to debate philosophy on the way. No one racing to a burning house pauses over a puzzle. And yet here we are — with our houses on fire in one way or another, our fears unaddressed, our better selves still waiting — and we are tying and untying knots.

Nature did not give us a generous allowance of time. We lose some to illness, some to the business of others, some to sleep, some to the ordinary friction of being alive. What remains, after all the subtractions, is not much. The question is what we do with it.

I have spent too much of mine on entertainment that wore the costume of inquiry. There is a difference between a mind that is healing itself and a mind that is distracting itself with beautiful problems. Philosophy used as a remedy and philosophy used as an aesthetic pleasure are not the same activity. I have done both, and I know which one leaves me changed at the end.

> Our minds are accustomed to entertain rather than to cure themselves — to make an aesthetic pleasure out of philosophy, when philosophy should really be a remedy.

So here is what I actually want. Not the distinction between wisdom and being wise. Not the subtle categorization of goods and non-goods and accessories to goods. What I want is this:

Make me braver. Make me calmer. Make me able to face what fortune brings without flinching, and when I do flinch — because I will — make me recover faster. Teach me to grieve without being destroyed by grief, to fail without being defined by failure, to hold the good things I have without the desperate grip that makes losing them catastrophic.

This is the work. Not the argument. Not the system. The actual interior renovation that means I am different next year than I am this year — steadier, freer, less at the mercy of whatever the day brings.

> Make me braver, make me calmer, make me the equal of Fortune, make me her superior.

I can be her superior. But not by spending the time I have on things that don't reach the place where the real trouble lives.
