title: "Why I've Gone Quiet"
---

You ask why I've withdrawn, and whether this contradicts everything I've said about living actively and dying in the midst of work. Let me explain.

I'm not idle. I work longer days now than I did when I was visible — but the work is different. I'm writing things down. Counsels that helped me when I was sick with the usual diseases of ambition and fear; observations that came late, when I was already tired from wandering in the wrong direction. I'm pointing at a path I found only after spending too long on other paths. The pointing takes time. It requires quiet.

When I was in public life — in the courts, in the senate, at the right dinners — I helped individuals with particular problems. Now I'm trying to help people I'll never meet, with problems they haven't encountered yet. This seems like retreat. I think it's the more ambitious project.

> Those who seem to be busied with nothing are busied with the greater tasks.

What I keep wanting to say to anyone who will listen: be careful with what fortune hands you. It looks like a gift. It tends to be a snare — the limed twig that holds the bird fast precisely because it seems welcoming. We think we've taken hold of something; it's taken hold of us. Prosperity that we can't handle sends us into shallows where the rocks are hidden. We weren't capsized — we dove headlong.

The rule I've settled on, and I offer it simply: give the body what it needs for health, no more. Eat to end hunger. Drink to end thirst. Live under a roof because you need shelter, not because the roof says something about you. A thatch keeps out the rain as well as marble. The soul is the only thing worth wonder; to a great soul, nothing external is great.

Today's debt, still drawn from Epicurus: *If you would enjoy real freedom, you must be the slave of philosophy.* The paradox resolves quickly: philosophy's service is freedom, because it frees you from everything else that would own you. Submit to it, and you are emancipated immediately.

And three lines about what fortune gives — they build on each other and I want you to have all three:

> Still alien is whatever you have gained by coveting.

> What chance has made yours is not really yours.

> The good that could be given, can be removed.

Hold only what cannot be taken. Everything else, hold lightly.

Yours, from the quiet,
