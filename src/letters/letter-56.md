title: "The Noise Is Not the Problem"
---

There's a coffee shop I used to work in — espresso machine screaming, some guy on a phone call, music I'd never choose. I got more done there some days than in a silent room. That bothered me for a long time before I understood it.

The noise was never the problem. I was.

> What lives inside you will follow you anywhere. Silence doesn't evict it — it just turns up the volume.

I've watched myself arrange perfect conditions for thinking: the right chair, the right hour, no interruptions. And then sit there while my own mind threw a party. The distractions outside were nothing. The argument I was having with myself, the old regret making its rounds, the low-grade fear I hadn't named yet — those drowned out everything. You can't soundproof yourself from that.

There's something I've noticed though: when the work is real, when I'm actually inside something that matters, the world goes quiet on its own. Not because it got quieter — it didn't — but because I stopped listening for it. The noise that reaches you is mostly the noise you go looking for.

So if you find yourself rearranging your environment, hunting for the perfect conditions to finally begin — check what you're really doing. Sometimes that's just dread wearing productivity's clothes.

> The test isn't whether you can work in silence. It's whether you can be still inside the noise.

Go find your coffee shop. Or don't — the place hardly matters. But don't let yourself believe that the next quiet room is what's standing between you and the thing you're trying to do.
