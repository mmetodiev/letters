I've been thinking about you. Asking people who know you, quietly, how you're doing. And honestly — the best thing I've heard is nothing. No one seems to know what you're up to lately. You've gone quiet, stayed close to yourself. That tells me more than any good report could.

I'm not worried about who you are. I trust that. What I think about is pace — how easily one person, even a well-meaning one, can slow you down without either of you noticing. Life is already short. We make it shorter by constantly starting over, abandoning one direction for another, breaking everything into fragments that never quite add up to something finished.

Here's an image I keep coming back to: imagine you're moving and you know something is closing in behind you. You wouldn't stop to weigh your options. You'd move. That's the energy I want for you — not panic, just clarity. The urgency of someone who understands that time doesn't wait, and that the goal isn't to avoid the end but to actually finish something before it arrives.

> A life isn't made better by being longer. It's made better by being complete.

I've noticed something about people who are always reaching toward the future — always needing more time, more chances, one more year to get it right. It usually means they haven't quite found themselves yet. Not their work, not their real work. A person who knows what they're for doesn't need tomorrow to fix today. The restlessness is the symptom. The cause is deeper.

I also want to say something about what the people who love you have wished for you over the years. The prosperity, the security, the success — all of it came from a good place. But I find myself wishing something different for you. Not less, exactly. Just different. I want you to reach a point where you don't need any of it to feel whole. Where you have enough self-possession that the absence of those things doesn't move you. That's a quieter wish, but I mean it more than any of the louder ones.

What I actually pray for — if that's not too strong a word — is that your mind settles. That the wandering stops. Not because you've given up, but because you've arrived somewhere inside yourself that feels solid. Where you know what actually matters to you, and you're living accordingly. Where you're not deferring the real life to some later version of yourself.

The person who has done that — who has genuinely completed something, filled it out, made it whole — is already free. Whatever comes next has nothing on him.

> He's earned his freedom — the one who still lives, but has already finished living well.

With love,
