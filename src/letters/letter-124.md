chapterNum: 124
title: "What Is Yours"
---

Here at the end of everything I've written to you, I want to say the plainest thing I know.

You are a reasoning animal. That is what you are, specifically, in the way that a fish is a swimming animal and an eagle is a flying one. Not the strongest creature, not the fastest, not the most beautiful — the horse outpaces you, the lion outgrows your hair, cattle outlift you without effort. In every competition that uses the body as its measure, you will lose to something better designed for that contest.

But reason. That is yours. That is where your Good lives, the only place it can live, because the Good is not a sensation — it cannot be tasted or touched or felt through the skin. It is what you arrive at when you have developed your nature all the way to its completion. Not pleasure, which you share with every living thing. Not comfort, which any creature seeks. Reason, brought to its fullest capacity: a mind that knows what is actually good and actually bad, that cannot be deceived by appearance, that is not at the mercy of what fortune chooses to offer or withhold.

I've spent these letters trying to say this in different ways, through different doors. Through the question of what we own and what owns us. Through grief and time and what death does and doesn't take. Through the company we keep, the education we receive, the style in which we speak, the way we arrange our hours. All of it has been circling this one thing: what is yours, really, in the deepest sense — what cannot be taken, what doesn't depend on circumstance, what grows stronger rather than more fragile as the years pass.

It is not the body. It is not the estate or the reputation or the position. It is not the carefully arranged life that fortune can disarrange in a night. It is the mind that has learned to see clearly — that can look at what everyone calls success and recognize it for what it often is, and look at what everyone calls disaster and recognize that too.

> You will come to your own when you understand that those whom the world calls fortunate are really the most unfortunate of all.

I do not say this to be cynical. I say it because I have watched enough of the fortunate to know that what they carry is heavier than what it looks like from outside. The anxiety of having, the labor of maintaining, the fear of losing, the discovery that arrival brings only the next departure — these are the hidden contents of the gift. Fortune gives with one hand and takes with the other, and the taking is always coming.

The person who has built their happiness on what reason provides, on the quality of their own mind and the steadiness of their own character — this person has something that fortune cannot adjust. Not because they are immune to difficulty, but because their foundation is not in the territory that difficulty can reach.

This is what I have been trying to give you. Not information, not argument, not even comfort in the usual sense — but a direction. A way of orienting yourself so that the losses, when they come, find you already standing on ground that holds.

We are parts of one body, you and I, and everything I've written has been written from that understanding — that your good is mine, that I have a stake in your becoming who you are capable of being. I'm not a finished product of what I've been saying. I'm someone who finds it easier to travel in this direction because you've been traveling alongside me.

Develop your reason. Let it be the measure of your joy. When you find that the things most people pursue have lost their pull on you — not through suppression, not through discipline, but because you actually see them for what they are — you will know you've arrived somewhere real.

Not at an end. At a beginning that holds.

Yours, entirely and without reservation,
