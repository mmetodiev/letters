chapterNum: 51
title: "Where you choose to be says something about who you are becoming."
---

I spent one night somewhere recently that I won't name, a place known for its beauty and its ease, where everything is designed for pleasure and nothing asks anything of you. I left the next morning. Not out of puritanism — I have no interest in making discomfort a virtue for its own sake. But I noticed something in myself that first evening that I didn't like. The softness of the place was already working on me. The effort of staying alert, of maintaining any kind of inner discipline, quietly doubled.

Environment is not neutral. We pretend it is, because we want the freedom to go anywhere without consequences. But places have a character, and that character gets into you whether you invite it or not.

There's a story about Hannibal that I keep coming back to. He crossed the Alps. He defeated Rome's armies in open battle, repeatedly, against odds that should have been impossible. And then he wintered in Campania — warm, comfortable, well-supplied — and something left him that never fully returned. He conquered with his weapons and was conquered by his ease.

> A single winter's comfort undid what years of hardship had built. The vices did what the mountains couldn't.

I'm not suggesting you live like a soldier. But I am suggesting you pay attention to what your surroundings are quietly asking of you — and whether you want to give it.

The deeper point is about pleasure specifically — not pleasure in general, but the kind that wraps itself around you and slowly removes your capacity for anything that requires effort. That kind of pleasure is not neutral either. Give yourself to it fully and you find you've also given yourself to its opposite: to pain when it comes, to anxiety, to the endless negotiation with appetite that never quite settles. Surrender to pleasure and you surrender to everything.

The freedom worth having — the only kind I find worth working toward — is not being a slave to any of it. Not comfort, not discomfort. Not pleasure, not its absence. The ability to be present with whatever is, without being owned by it.

> Animals hardened on rough ground can travel any road. Those fattened on soft meadows wear out quickly. The same is true of character.

Choose your surroundings with some of the same care you give your thinking. Not austerity for its own sake — but conditions that keep you honest. That ask something of you. That don't make it too easy to forget what you're working toward.

Vice has no limit and no end. You have to be the one who sets them.

Yours, watching the environment carefully,
