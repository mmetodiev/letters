title: "What Are You Aiming At?"
--- 

There's a question I keep coming back to, and I want to put it to you plainly: what is the whole thing *for*? Not this decision or that one — the whole of it. Because I notice, in myself and in the people I love most, that we spend enormous energy on the parts and almost none on the shape of the life they're supposed to add up to.

The archer doesn't just practice drawing the bow. He has to know what he's pointing at. Without that, every shot is just motion.

> When you don't know what harbour you're making for, no wind is the right wind.

I've watched you work hard at things that clearly matter to you. But I wonder sometimes if the things you're working toward are things you actually chose, or things that accumulated around you until they felt like a destination. There's a difference, and it shows — not always in the results, but in whether you feel like yourself while you're getting there.

Here's what I believe, and it's the kind of thing that sounds simple until you sit with it: the only thing that's genuinely good is acting with integrity — with virtue, if you'll forgive the old word. Not success. Not comfort. Not even happiness as most people describe it. Those things can be taken from you. The quality of your choosing cannot be.

> What virtue touches, it makes good — even the hard things, even the losses.

I know how that sounds. I know it can read like the kind of advice that's easy to give from the outside. But I'm not outside this. I've had stretches where I couldn't tell you what I was aiming at either, and I felt it — that low, persistent wrongness of living by drift. The goal isn't some distant peak. Seneca says it stands right beside us, and I think he's right. We just keep looking past it.

So: what's the harbour? You don't have to answer me. But answer yourself, honestly, soon. Everything else follows from that — or it doesn't follow at all.
