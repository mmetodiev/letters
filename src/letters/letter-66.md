title: "Virtue Doesn't Come in Degrees"
---

I want to tell you about someone I know — not young, not conventionally impressive, body giving them real trouble — who is one of the finest people I've ever met. You'd walk past them on the street and see none of it. That's the point.

We keep looking for virtue in the wrong places. In the polished version, the one with the advantages, the one whose goodness costs them nothing visible. But that's not where it lives. Or rather — it lives there too, but you can't see it as clearly.

> Virtue doesn't improve in favorable conditions. It only becomes more visible in difficult ones.

Here's what I've been working through: if the good is genuinely good, then it can't be more or less good depending on the circumstances surrounding it. Courage in a comfortable life and courage on the rack are the same courage. The rack just makes it legible. One straight line is not straighter than another straight line because it runs through rougher terrain.

I know what you'll say — surely joy is better than suffering, surely peace is preferable to torment. Yes. Obviously. I'm not arguing for seeking pain. I'm arguing that what you do inside the pain is the same quality of thing as what you do inside the joy. The virtue is equal. The circumstances are just circumstances.

What this means practically: stop rating your own goodness by how easy things have been lately. The person you are under pressure is not a lesser version of you. And the person you are when everything is fine is not inflated.

> You are not more yourself when life cooperates. You are just more comfortable. The self is the same.

I see you clearly in both conditions. I want you to do the same.

Yours, with equal admiration in all weather —
