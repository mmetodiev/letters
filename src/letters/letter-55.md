chapterNum: 55
title: "Hiding is not the same as living quietly."
---

I passed a place today that made me think — a villa belonging to a man who was famous, in his time, for nothing except having successfully withdrawn from everything. When the political storms came and took people down, when being close to the wrong person meant ruin, everyone would point to him and say: that man knows how to live. He survived by making himself invisible. He outlasted all of them.

But every time I used to pass his house I thought the same thing: here lies a man who is already buried. He wasn't living quietly. He was hiding. And those are not the same thing at all.

> Leisure belongs only to the person who has chosen it freely, from a place of wholeness. Idleness forced by fear or disappointment is just a different kind of prison.

The person who retreats from the world because the world has hurt them, who withdraws because they cannot bear to see others doing better, who hides because they are afraid — that person is not living for themselves. They are living for their comfort, their resentments, their unexamined fears. The solitude looks like philosophy from the outside. From the inside it is just avoidance with better scenery.

The villa itself was beautiful, by the way. I could see that much from the road. Cleverly positioned, sheltered from the wind, private without being remote. A place well-chosen for the wrong reasons by a man who understood comfort and nothing deeper. It is possible to arrange your life with great sophistication around the wrong centre.

But here is what stayed with me, walking back: the place where you live contributes almost nothing to tranquillity. I have known people in beautiful surroundings who were miserable, and people in difficult ones who were completely at peace. The mind makes the place, not the other way around. You already know this. I say it because it's easy to forget when the surroundings are particularly good or particularly bad.

I've been thinking about you today, in the way I often do when I'm walking somewhere alone. You were present in my thoughts as clearly as if you were beside me. That kind of presence — the friend carried in the mind, available at any moment — is something I've come to value more than physical proximity. Presence makes us careless with each other. We take for granted what we can always reach for. Distance sharpens attention.

> A friend retained in the spirit can never be absent. I see you, I hear you — and at this distance, perhaps more clearly than I would face to face.
