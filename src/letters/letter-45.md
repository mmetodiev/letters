title: "Stop perfecting the argument. Start living the answer"

Stop perfecting the argument. Start living the answer.
You mentioned wanting more books, more reading, more input. I understand the impulse — I feel it too. But I've come to think that a short list of things that genuinely matter does more than a vast library of things that merely interest. The problem isn't scarcity of ideas. It's that we keep adding to the pile instead of living what we've already understood.

Read widely if you want. But follow one road toward the thing that actually matters. Wandering through many paths isn't travelling — it's just movement.

There's a kind of thinking I want to name, because I see it everywhere and I think it's one of the more elegant ways we waste ourselves. It's the habit of getting very good at making distinctions that don't actually matter — of sharpening arguments about words while the real questions about how to live and how to die go unasked. Clever people are especially vulnerable to this. The thinking feels productive. It exercises something. But it goes nowhere.

> We don't yet know how to live. We don't yet know how to die. These are the things that deserve our whole attention — not the knots we tie in language for the pleasure of untying them.

It's things that lead us astray, not words. And the real confusions — the ones that cost us — are between things that look alike but aren't. Flattery and friendship. Rashness and courage. Cowardice dressed up as prudence. These are the resemblances worth learning to see through, because going wrong here carries real consequences.

If you want to do the work that actually matters, here is where to put your attention: the question of what a happy life really looks like, as opposed to what the world says it looks like. Not the person with the largest accumulation, the most impressive position, the fullest room. The person who is upright and unshaken. Who wants nothing that requires him to diminish himself. Who is moved by difficulty but never undone by it. Whom Fortune, even at her most violent, can graze but never wound.

> Her missiles, the ones that flatten most people, simply bounce off such a person — like hail rattling on a roof, doing nothing to the one inside.

And underneath all of this is the most practical point of all, the one I keep returning to. Most people are not living. They are preparing to live. Everything meaningful is deferred to a future that keeps receding. The instruments of life are accumulated at the expense of life itself. Even if we paid strict attention, time would outrun us. As it is, we barely look up.

Life doesn't wait. It passes by as if it belonged to someone else — and though it ends on the final day, it is lost a little every day we spend preparing for it instead of inside it.

Yours, with no patience for the hairsplitters,
