title: "I Have Nothing to Do With You, Fortune"
---

There is a particular freedom I've been tasting lately, and I want to describe it to you because I think you might want it too.

Imagine standing at the edge of a crowd where everyone is competing for something — position, recognition, the approval of the right people, the prize that will finally make things feel settled. Everyone straining, everyone anxious, everyone performing for an audience that is itself performing. And you are simply... not competing. Not because you've given up, or because you're too proud, or because you've decided nothing matters. But because you've genuinely looked at what they're chasing and found it not worth chasing.

That's the freedom I mean. Not withdrawal — you're still there, still present, still engaged with the world. But unhooked from the outcome. Fortune is running her elections, handing out her consulships and her prizes and her reversals, and you have nothing in the ballot.

> Fortune, I have nothing to do with you. I am not at your service.

I know how this sounds. It can sound like defeat dressed up as philosophy, like the person who didn't get invited to the party explaining why they didn't want to go. But that's not what I'm describing. I'm describing what happens when you've actually achieved the wanting — gotten the thing, or enough things like it to know their weight — and discovered that the satisfaction was briefer and thinner than the pursuit. Nobody who has reached what they were climbing toward is satisfied. The top turns out to be just another rung. The prize recedes.

This is not pessimism. It's precision. Happiness is not a large thing; it is a close one. It doesn't loom from a distance because it doesn't live at a distance. The man who looks at it from far away sees it as enormous; the man who reaches it finds it modest and immediate. The scramble was never for what it appeared to be for.

Here's the image I keep returning to: an archway. The stones lean toward each other and would fall without support — until the keystone is placed, the final piece, the one that holds everything together by occupying the exact middle. Before that stone, you have leaning stones. After it, you have an arch. The keystone doesn't add much in size. What it adds transforms the nature of what exists.

This is how character works, and how wisdom works. Small accumulations, each one seeming minor, and then at some point the quality changes. What was almost enough becomes enough. What was a collection of improvements becomes something with a different name entirely. The quantity tipped into a new kind of thing.

This is why I keep writing, and why I ask you to keep reading and thinking and returning to these questions even when the progress seems slow. You are placing stones. The arch isn't visible yet. But it's coming, and when the last piece finds its place, you'll know it not because something was added but because everything that was already there finally held.
