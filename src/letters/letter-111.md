title: "The Mountain Doesn't Stretch Itself"
--- 

There's a particular kind of cleverness that feels like progress and isn't. You know what I mean — the intricate argument, the neat paradox, the syllogism that closes on itself like a trap. There's real pleasure in it. The mind loves this work the way it loves any puzzle, and the pleasure is clean enough that it's easy to mistake for the other thing: actually becoming someone better.

It isn't. I've spent enough time in both places to know the difference from the inside. The philosophical puzzle leaves you exactly where you were, just more nimble. The genuine work — the kind that costs something — leaves you changed. Less afraid, or more honest, or quieter in the ways that matter. Slower to be rattled. More yourself under pressure.

The person who has actually done this work has a particular quality that's hard to fake: they get more impressive as you get closer. Most impressive things work the other way — they're designed to look large from a distance and reveal their limitations up close. But a mountain is genuinely taller than it appears from far away, and a person who has been quietly doing the real work for years turns out to be more than they seemed. No performance. No stretching onto tiptoe to appear taller.

> Fortune cannot reach her hands to such a height. Equal to himself under all conditions — whether the current runs free or whether he travels desperate seas.

I'm not telling you to avoid the clever exercises entirely. Play with them when you want to play. But don't let them crowd out what actually matters, which is the single, difficult, lifelong work of learning to hold your own life lightly enough that it doesn't hold you.

And here — carefully — is the distinction I want to leave you with, because I mean it precisely: the goal is not to *control* your life. Control is the second task. The first is to *despise* it — not with contempt, but with a freedom from desperate attachment, a willingness to lose it, a settled knowledge that it is not the most important thing about you.

Only from that ground can you actually begin to live it well.
