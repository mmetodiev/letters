chapterNum: 31
title: "Stop up your ears — even to the ones who love you."
---


I see you. I mean that. The person you've been moving toward — the one you promised yourself you'd become — I can see them taking shape. Don't stop now. Don't let anything pull you back toward the crowd's idea of a good life. You've already laid more foundation than most people ever do. Just finish what you started.

But here's the thing about sirens: Ulysses only had to sail past one headland. You have to sail past the whole world. And the most dangerous voices aren't the ones that sound obviously wrong. They're the ones that come from people who genuinely love you, who want good things for you, and who have no idea that what they're wishing for would cost you everything that actually matters. Be kind to them. And be deaf to them.

> If you want to be happy, pray that none of their fondest wishes for you come true.

That sounds harsh. It isn't. It's just that the things people wish for the ones they love — comfort, status, security, recognition — are not the same as the things that make a life. They're decorations at best. At worst they're the very weights that keep you from rising.

The only real good is trust in yourself. Not confidence in the ordinary sense — not the belief that things will go well. The deeper thing: knowing that whatever comes, you can meet it. That you are not at the mercy of circumstances because you've built something inside yourself that circumstances can't reach.

And that kind of self-possession only comes through work. Not suffering for its own sake — pointless toil is pointless. But the work of stretching toward something honourable, of refusing to halt halfway up the hill. That work doesn't diminish you. It's what you're made of.

> Work is the sustenance of noble minds. Ask for it. Don't just endure it.

I want to say something about what you're actually reaching for, underneath all of this. It isn't wealth — God has no property. It isn't reputation — God is unknown to most people and doesn't suffer for it. It isn't beauty, strength, position, or the size of the room that falls quiet when you enter. All of that fades. All of it passes, sooner than you expect, under the control of forces you cannot resist.

What doesn't pass is the soul — the soul that is upright, and good, and large. That is the thing worth building. And here is what I find quietly extraordinary about this: it is available to everyone. It doesn't belong to any rank or background or circumstance. One may leap to heaven from the very slums. The gods themselves, when they chose to walk among us, were moulded in clay.

You don't have to travel anywhere to find this. Nature has already equipped you. The journey is safe and the road is inside you. The only question is whether you'll prove true to what you've been given.

I believe you will.

Yours, without reservation,
