chapterNum: 52
title: "You cannot do this alone. Choose carefully who helps you."
---

Have you ever noticed how you can want something sincerely — a change, a direction, a way of being — and still find yourself pulled the other way? Not by weakness exactly. By something more stubborn than that. The will goes one direction and the self goes another, and no amount of resolution quite closes the gap.

This is not a character flaw. It is just the condition. Almost no one escapes it by sheer force of individual will. The ones who do are rare enough that we shouldn't plan around being one of them. The rest of us need help — a hand extended at the right moment, a voice that says keep going when the inner voice has gone quiet.

There is no shame in this. The building constructed on difficult ground, that had to sink its foundations through soft earth to reach solid rock — that building required more work, more material, more patience. But it stands on something real. The one built on firm ground from the start was easier, but perhaps the builder learned less. I find I have more respect for the person who had to fight for every inch of solid ground than for the one who arrived there without friction.

> It is a greater credit to have brought about the same result with the more difficult material. Don't despise the difficulty. It is doing something.

So — who do you turn to? I'd say this: don't limit yourself to the living. The people whose thinking has survived centuries did so because it was worth surviving. They have time for you in a way that most living people don't. Go to them. Let them be your helpers.

Among the living, though, choose carefully. Not the ones who perform wisdom — who speak beautifully, who draw crowds, who leave everyone feeling uplifted and changed and who somehow change nothing. The appetite for applause is not compatible with the kind of teaching that actually works. A surgeon operating on you is not looking for your approval. Neither should the person helping you think more clearly.

> Choose as a guide someone you will admire more when you see them act than when you hear them speak.

That is the whole test. Everything else — the eloquence, the reputation, the size of the audience — is secondary at best and misleading at worst. The teacher worth following is the one whose life is the argument. Who does what they say. Who is caught, consistently, being exactly who they claimed to be when no one was watching.

Those people are rare. But they exist. And they are the ones who will actually help you close the gap between the person you intend to be and the person you are.

Yours, still looking for the right guides,
