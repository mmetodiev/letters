title: "You matter more than you think — so live accordingly"
---

I know more about what you've been thinking than you might expect. Not because I have any special intelligence — just because the world is smaller than we imagine, and people talk. Don't dismiss this with "I'm not important enough to be noticed." That's the wrong measure. Importance is always relative to where you're standing. A ship that looks large on a river looks small at sea. You are where you are, and where you are, you are seen.

People are watching — how you spend your time, how you treat others, whether who you are in public and who you are in private are the same person. This isn't meant to make you anxious. It's meant to be clarifying. You already know the standard you want to live by. The question is whether you're living by it when you think no one is looking.

> You will know you have arrived somewhere real when you could leave the door open and not be afraid of what anyone might see.

Think about that honestly. Most of us keep walls not for safety but for concealment. We tell ourselves we value privacy. What we often mean is that we value the gap between our public self and our private one — and we need the walls to protect it. That gap is worth examining. Not with shame. With curiosity.

A clear conscience doesn't hide from the crowd — it's comfortable in it. A troubled one is restless even in solitude. You can close every door, go somewhere no one knows your name, and still not escape the one witness you can never leave behind.

If what you're doing is good, let it be seen. If it isn't — the darkness doesn't help you. You already know. And you are not a witness worth despising.

> How wretched to live well only in public, and how equally wretched to sin only in private — as if the one who matters most isn't always in the room.

Watching, with love and without judgment,
