title: "What You Knew Before You Were Taught"
---

Here is something worth noticing: the young animal knows what will hurt it before it has any experience of being hurt. The newly hatched chick doesn't flee the peacock — a large and colorful bird — but scatters the moment a hawk's shadow crosses the ground. It has never encountered a hawk. Nobody taught it the difference. The knowledge arrived with the creature, built in before experience could confirm it.

I think about this when I consider how much of what matters we know before we're told. The infant who hasn't yet learned to speak already understands discomfort and seeks comfort, already has preferences, already reaches toward what is good for it and recoils from what isn't. Before language, before argument, before any of the machinery of conscious thought — the orientation was already there.

We call this instinct, and then we use the word to dismiss it, as if something that doesn't require effort doesn't count. But the spider's web is as precisely engineered as anything human hands have made — more precisely, actually, because every web is equally fine, and every cell of honeycomb is identical in shape. There is no variation between the expert and the novice spider, because the skill isn't acquired. It was given. Nature's assignments are uniform; what art teaches is uncertain and uneven.

> No animal is unskilled in the use of its body. They come into the world with this knowledge; they are born full-trained.

What does this mean for us? We arrived with something too. An original orientation toward our own good, a native sense of what fits us and what doesn't, a constitution we were born already adapted to. The question isn't whether we have this — we do. The question is whether we've buried it under everything that came after.

The child learning to walk falls and rises, falls and rises, and doesn't need to be told to try again. Something in it insists. The turtle flipped onto its back struggles toward its proper position not because it reasons through the problem but because being inverted is simply wrong, and the body knows it before the mind does. This is self-love in its original form — not vanity, not narcissism, but the basic, unkillable insistence of a living thing on its own continuation and proper functioning.

We're equipped with this. Then we spend decades learning to override it — to ignore what the body knows, to dismiss what the instinct recognizes, to accept arrangements that feel wrong because someone with authority told us they were right. We learn to tolerate what hurts and to chase what empties us. We become, in some ways, less reliable than the chick that knows the hawk's shadow without ever having seen a hawk.

The beginning of recovery is returning to what was always there underneath the accumulation. You already know, before any philosophy, what fits you and what doesn't. You already know, at some level below argument, what threatens the thing you are. The question is whether you'll trust that knowledge — whether you'll let the original equipment do the work it was designed to do, rather than overriding it constantly with noise and desire and the opinions of people who don't know you.

Every living thing is adapted to its own constitution. Be adapted to yours.
