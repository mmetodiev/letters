title: "What Got Me Through"
--- 

I want to tell you about the time I was genuinely ill — not a bad week, but the kind of sick where you start doing the arithmetic on whether you want to continue. I won't dramatize it. But I want you to know it happened, because what I learned from it is the most useful thing I can offer you when you're suffering.

Philosophy kept me alive. Not as a slogan — as a literal fact. When the body becomes a problem to be managed, the mind is either your refuge or your second enemy. I chose refuge. I read, I thought, I wrote. I stayed inside ideas when I couldn't stay inside anything else. And somehow that was enough.

> The fear of death is not a fear of disease. It is a fear of nature. Even when you recover, the same end still awaits you — you will have escaped illness, not mortality.

Here is what I know about pain, from the inside: it is almost never as continuous as you fear it will be. It has gaps. It dulls the very nerves it tortures. The worst of it tends to be sharp and short; what endures is milder. And then there is this — that suffering is half interpretation. Not all of it, I won't insult you with that, but half. The story you tell yourself about the pain, the weight you add by cataloguing it and rehearsing it — that part is optional. You can put it down.

What also helped me, and I mean this plainly: knowing people would grieve me. I stayed for them before I stayed for myself. There's no shame in that. Sometimes love is the rope when reason isn't enough.

> A single day among the learned lasts longer than the longest life of the ignorant. Time thickens when you're actually using it.

So here is my counsel, which is the same thing philosophy taught me in that sickroom: don't add to what's already hard. Don't borrow tomorrow's suffering to stack on top of today's. Don't retell yesterday's pain as though it's still happening. You are here, now, in this moment — which contains both the difficulty and the capacity to meet it. That capacity is yours. It doesn't get sick.

Yours, from someone who came back —
