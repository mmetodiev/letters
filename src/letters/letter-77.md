title: "The Play Doesn't Have to Be Long"
--- 

I watched the ships come into the harbour today and felt, for a moment, completely at peace with having nowhere to be. No news I urgently needed. No outcome I was breathlessly awaiting. Just the water and the sails and the fact of being alive, which is not nothing.

I want to say something about death. Not darkly — I'm not writing to you from a dark place. I'm writing because I think we spend enormous effort not saying this, and the not-saying doesn't protect us from anything.

> At whatever point you leave off living, provided you leave off nobly, your life is a whole.

There was a man Seneca knew — Marcellinus — who was ill and tired and decided, calmly, that he was finished. He distributed gifts to his household. He climbed into a warm bath. He let himself dissolve gently over three days, remarking near the end that it wasn't unpleasant. I find this, strange as it sounds, deeply comforting. Not as a template, but as a proof that the ending doesn't have to be a catastrophe. That it's possible to close a life the way you'd close a book you loved — carefully, at the right moment, with a hand on the cover.

What I notice in myself, and maybe in you: we treat longevity as though it were the point. As though a life of ninety years is simply better than a life of fifty, the way a longer rope is better than a shorter one. But it isn't rope. It's a play. The question is never how long — it's whether the thing had shape, whether it meant something, whether you were actually in it or just running out the clock.

> It is with life as it is with a play: it matters not how long the action is spun out, but how good the acting is.

I don't want you to die. I want you to live so fully that the length becomes secondary — that whenever it ends, it will have been enough. That's different from clinging. Clinging is what you do when you're not sure you've really lived yet, when you keep hoping the real part is still coming.

The real part is now. You know this. So do I.

With love, and without fear —
