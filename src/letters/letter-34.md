title: "You make me feel young again."
---
I don't say this lightly, and I don't say it to flatter you. When I see what you're becoming — the choices you're making, the way you carry yourself, the distance you've already put between who you were and who you are now — something in me lifts. Genuinely lifts. The years fall away a little. I feel it the way I imagine a gardener feels watching something he planted finally come into fruit. Not surprise exactly. More like — relief. And joy.

I want to claim some small part of you. Not ownership — nothing like that. Just the quiet pride of having been nearby when you were figuring things out. Of having believed in what I saw before you fully believed in it yourself. Of having pushed when you needed pushing and stayed close when you needed that instead.

> Watching you grow has made me grow too. That's not something I expected.

You might ask what more I could possibly want from you at this point. Honestly? Very little. The will is already there — and the will is almost everything. More than half, I'd say. The kind of goodness worth having isn't a skill you acquire. It lives in the decision to keep becoming it. Every day, quietly, without needing applause.

A good person — the kind I think you're becoming — is someone who is complete. Finished in the best sense. Someone whose character holds under pressure, who can't be cheapened by circumstance or need. That's not a ceiling. It's a kind of freedom.

The one thing I'll ask: keep your actions and your words in the same register. Let them come from the same place. When what a person does and what a person says drift apart, something inside them goes crooked — slowly, quietly, but it does. You've worked too hard for that.

> Stay whole. Everything else will follow.
