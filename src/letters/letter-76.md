title: "It Is Never Too Late to Learn How to Live"
--- 

I want to tell you something that might embarrass me a little, which is how you'll know I mean it: I am still learning basic things. Not advanced things. Basic ones. How to be present. How to act from what I actually believe rather than from habit or fear. How to want the right things.

I used to think that by a certain age you were supposed to have this sorted. You're not supposed to still be figuring out what a good life looks like. And yet here I am, and here, I suspect, are you.

> Wisdom comes to no one by accident. Money might, reputation might, even love might — but not this.

What Seneca keeps returning to, and what I keep finding true, is that the only quality worth measuring in a person is the quality of their reason — how well ordered it is, how honest, how free from distortion. Everything else we admire in people — their resources, their charm, their status — is like gilding on a sword. What matters is the edge. A sword is good when it cuts cleanly, not when the scabbard is jewelled. A person is good when their judgment is sound, not when their circumstances are impressive.

I think of people I know who have everything arranged correctly from the outside and are miserable inside, and people with almost nothing who are somehow entirely themselves. You already know which kind you'd rather be. You've always known. The question is just whether we're building toward it or only admiring it from a distance.

> Measure the person, not the pedestal. A dwarf on a mountain is still a dwarf. A great soul in a small life is still great.

The other thing this letter says, and I don't want to glide past it: think now about what you'd die for. Not morbidly — as a way of getting clear. If you know what you'd give everything for, you know what you actually value. The person who would lay down their life without hesitation for something honourable has already answered the question of what the good is. Their body knows what their philosophy is still working out.

I'm still working it out too. But I'm working.

With love, and without embarrassment —
